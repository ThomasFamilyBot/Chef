Red wine-balsamic jus, lacquered edges, sharp herbs & Noma Pumpkin Bushi. Served on lamb-fat Yorkshires.

The shoulder is dry-brined on Day 1, smoked gently over charcoal and restrained post oak on Day 2, then chilled. On Day 3 it is braised with the smoked onions, shallots and garlic until completely tender. The braising liquid becomes a concentrated red wine-balsamic jus; the lamb is kept in large chunks, lightly lacquered and crisped at high heat. Smoke stays in the background: this should eat like an exceptional braised lamb dish, not barbecue.

**Day 1 — Dry-brine** (active ~25 min; refrigerate 18-24 hours)

**Dry-brine the lamb**

- Trim: pat the shoulder dry. Remove loose flaps, silverskin and any hard exterior fat, but leave an even fat cap up to ~1/4". Do not debone. Lightly score only a thick fat cap; do not cut into the meat.
- Mix 25 g Diamond Crystal kosher salt with 2 tsp black pepper. Apply all over, working into seams and around the bone (~0.9% salt for a 6 lb shoulder).
- Place fat-side up on a wire rack over a rimmed tray. Refrigerate uncovered 18-24 hours.
- Optional mise for tomorrow: quarter the onions, halve the shallots and the garlic head crosswise; store sealed and cold. Keep the oak dry. Group the wine, stock, balsamic and herbs for Day 3 but don't combine them. Wash and dry the mint and parsley no earlier than Day 2.
- Day 1 endpoint: surface looks seasoned and dry, not wet or tacky. No rinsing before smoking.

**Day 2 — Smoke for color and complexity** (active ~45 min; smoking and cooling ~4 1/2-5 1/2 hours)

**Smoke the lamb and aromatics**

- T+0:00 — Light a two-zone charcoal fire (Slow 'N Sear); stabilize the kettle at 250-275°F.
- T+0:35 — Once the charcoal is burning cleanly, add only 2 small post oak chunks. Thin, nearly invisible smoke is the target; avoid billowing white smoke.
- Set the shoulder fat-side up on the indirect side, thickest end toward the heat, lid vent over the lamb. Rotate only if one side colors much faster.
- Lightly oil the onions, shallots and garlic. Place cut-side exposed in a shallow heatproof pan under the grate to catch lamb drippings. Pull after 60-90 min, when bronze at the edges and softened; light tent ~1 hr, then close the foil and refrigerate once cooled.
- T+3:30 — Begin checking lamb color and internal temperature.
- T+4:00-4:30 — Pull when deep bronze-mahogany, gently smoky, and roughly 155-165°F internally. Cook for color, not tenderness; tenderness comes in tomorrow's braise.
- Rest 10-15 min, then move to a shallow tray and refrigerate uncovered until cold; cover once fully chilled. Refrigerate within 2 hours of leaving the smoker. Store the aromatics separately.
- Optional: strain and chill any clean rendered lamb fat separately for the Yorkshire puddings. Don't mix smoky drippings into the jus unless they taste clean.

**Day 3 — Braise, hand off, reheat and lacquer** (schedule assumes a 1-hour travel break)

*At your house*

**Braise**

- T+0:00 — Heat oven to 300°F. Set the cold smoked shoulder in a snug Dutch oven with the smoked onions, shallots, garlic, rosemary, thyme and bay. Add wine, balsamic and enough stock to reach halfway to two-thirds up the meat.
- T+0:20-3:50 — Cover tightly and braise at 300°F ~3 1/2 hours, turning once, until a skewer meets little resistance and the bone moves freely.

**Rest, chunk and reduce the jus**

- T+3:50-4:35 — Rest lamb 30-45 min, loosely covered. Strain the liquid, pressing lightly on the aromatics; discard solids. Defat thoroughly.
- Chunk; do not shred. Remove the bone and large pockets of fat. Break along natural seams into substantial 1 1/2-2" pieces.
- Reduce and fully season the jus, but hold the butter: boil (212°F) the defatted liquid in a wide pan until glossy and able to coat a spoon, about 1 1/2-2 cups. Adjust with red wine vinegar, salt and pepper. It should taste slightly intense.
- T+4:40 — Toss the chunks with only 2-4 TBLS jus. Lamb in a shallow covered container, remaining jus in a separate leakproof container. Rapid-cool (ice bath for the jus if helpful); pack at 40°F or colder in an insulated cooler with ample ice packs. If hot-holding instead, keep at 140°F or hotter throughout.

*At your friend's house*

**Reheat and make the herb garnish**

- T+7:10-7:35 — Reheat the lamb covered at ~300°F with a few tablespoons of jus until thoroughly hot, 15-25 min (to 165°F). Reheat the jus gently.
- Toss chopped mint, parsley and minced shallot with ~1 TBLS red wine vinegar, the olive oil and salt just before serving.
- With one oven: reheat the lamb before the Yorkshires go in, then hold it covered. When the Yorkshires come out, switch immediately to 475-500°F or broil.

**Lacquer, finish the jus and serve**

- T+7:50-8:00 — Arrange hot lamb in one layer on a rimmed sheet pan. Brush with a thin film of jus; blast 5-10 min, turning and brushing once, until edges are dark, sticky and crisp. Watch continuously.
- Take the warm jus off the heat and whisk in the cold butter a few cubes at a time.
- Plate Yorkshire pudding, lamb and jus; add the herb garnish and finely grate Pumpkin Bushi over the top. Serve immediately.
