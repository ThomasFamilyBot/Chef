**1 to 3 days ahead: salt the chicken**

- Season 1-3 days before serving; for a 3 1/4-3 1/2 lb bird, at least 2 days. Remove the lump of fat inside and pat the chicken very dry: a wet chicken steams before it browns.
- Slide a finger under the skin of each breast to make two pockets, then loosen a pocket over the thickest part of each thigh. Push an herb sprig into each of the 4 pockets.
- Season liberally all over with about 3/4 tsp sea salt per pound and the pepper; a little salt inside the cavity and on the backbone. Twist and tuck the wing tips behind the shoulders. Cover loosely and refrigerate.

**Roast**

- Heat the oven to 475°F. You may need anywhere from 450°F to 500°F depending on your oven and the bird.
- Choose a shallow flameproof roasting pan barely larger than the chicken, or a 10-inch skillet with a metal handle. Preheat it over medium heat (pan surface 300-350°F). Wipe the chicken dry and set it breast up in the pan; it should sizzle.
- Roast in the center of the oven. It should start sizzling and browning within 20 minutes; if not, raise the heat. The skin should blister; if it chars or the fat smokes, lower the heat 25°F.
- After about 30 minutes, flip the bird (a dry bird and a hot pan keep the skin from sticking). Roast 10-20 minutes more, then flip back to re-crisp the breast, 5-10 minutes. Total 45 minutes to 1 hour; thigh 170°F+ at the joint.

**Pan juices**

- Move the chicken to a plate. Pour off the clear fat, leaving the drippings. Add about 1 TBLS water to the hot pan and swirl.
- Slash the stretched skin between thighs and breasts, then tilt the bird over the pan to drain its juices into the drippings. Skim the last of the fat.
- Set the pan over medium-low heat, add any juice from under the resting chicken, and bring to a simmer, stirring and scraping.
- Cut the chicken into pieces and pour the pan juices over.
