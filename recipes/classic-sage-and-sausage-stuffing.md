**Dry the bread**

- Set the oven racks to lower-middle and upper-middle. Heat the oven to 275°F.
- Spread the bread from `1 -> Bread` over 2 rimmed baking sheets. Bake until completely dried, about 50 minutes, rotating the trays and stirring the cubes several times.
- Let the bread cool. Raise the oven to 350°F.

**Cook the sausage base**

- In a large Dutch oven, melt the butter over medium-high heat (pan surface 375-425°F) until the foaming subsides, about 2 minutes. Don't let the butter color.
- Add the sausage and mash it with a stiff whisk or potato masher into fine pieces, none bigger than 1/4 inch. Cook, stirring often, until only a few bits of pink remain, about 8 minutes.
- Add the onion, celery, garlic and sage. Cook, stirring often, until the vegetables are soft, about 10 minutes.
- Take the pot off the heat and stir in half of the stock (2 cups). With low-sodium or homemade stock, season with salt and pepper now.

**Add the egg mixture and bread**

- Whisk the remaining 2 cups stock, the eggs and 3 TBLS of the parsley until completely blended.
- Stirring constantly with a wooden spoon, slowly pour the egg mixture into the sausage mixture.
- Add the dried bread and fold gently until evenly mixed.

**Bake**

- Transfer to a buttered 9-by-13-inch baking dish, or a 10-by-14-inch oval. Cover tightly with foil.
- Bake until the center reads 150°F on an instant-read thermometer, about 45 minutes.
- Remove the foil and bake until the top is crisp and well colored, 15 to 20 minutes more.

**Finish**

- Let it cool 5 minutes, sprinkle with the remaining parsley and serve.

**Notes**

- Make ahead: mix everything and put it in the buttered dish a day in advance. Take it out of the fridge at least 1 hour before baking so it comes to room temperature.
- Half batch: halve everything but use 2 eggs. Dry the bread on 1 baking sheet and bake the stuffing in a 10-inch cast iron or carbon steel skillet.
- Go by the thermometer, not the clock. Reviewers on the recipe page report the covered bake running both short and long.
- Short on bread: scale the stock to the bread, 0.8 fl oz of stock for each 1 oz of fresh bread. A 31.5 oz loaf takes 3 cups plus 2 TBLS, half in the sausage base and half in the egg mixture. Keep the 3 eggs and everything else as written, and start checking for 150°F at about 35 minutes.
- Why oven-dried bread: bread dried in a low oven absorbs more stock than stale bread, which is how 2 loaves take a full quart.
- For a less meaty version the author says sage alone works. A small dish made without the sausage, with vegetable stock, covers vegetarians.
- It also stuffs a bird. The full batch is enough for two or three large turkeys, with the rest baked in a dish.
- Dried or fresh fruit and nuts can be folded in with the bread.
