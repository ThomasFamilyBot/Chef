**Prep & dry brine**

- Rinse the slab in cool water to clear bone bits. If the membrane is still on the bone side, slide a butter knife under it, work a section loose, grip with a paper towel and peel it off (or slash it every inch if it won't come).
- Trim excess fat from both sides.
- Salt at about 1/4 tsp kosher salt per lb (ribs are about half bone). Give it 1-2 hours to absorb if you can (dry brining).

**Memphis Dust**

- Grind the rosemary to a powder, then mix everything from `2 -> Memphis Dust (full batch, about 2 1/2 cups)`, breaking up sugar lumps. It keeps for months in a tight jar. Recipe: [Meathead's Memphis Dust](https://amazingribs.com/tested-recipes/spice-rubs-and-pastes/meatheads-memphis-dust-rub-recipe/).
- Wet the ribs lightly with water and sprinkle on about 2 TBLS per side: enough to coat, with the meat still showing through. Rub it in.

**Smoke**

- Set the smoker, or the grill for two-zone indirect cooking, to 225°F and hold it there; stay between 200°F and 250°F. Use a digital thermometer at grate level, not the lid dial.
- Add about 4 oz dry wood as close to the fire as possible. Put the ribs on the indirect side, meaty side up, and close the lid.
- When the smoke dwindles after 20-30 minutes, add the other 4 oz of wood. No more after that.
- Cook 5-7 hours for St. Louis or spare ribs, 3-5 for baby backs. With more than one slab, swap the near and far slabs halfway; no need to flip.
- Done when they pass the bend test: lift the slab with tongs and bounce it; the surface cracks. With a thin probe away from bone, about 200°F.

**Sauce & sizzle**

- Optional: paint both sides lightly with the sauce and cook 15 minutes more. Not earlier, or the sugar burns.
- Then sizzle it: move the slab directly over the hottest part of the fire, lid open, and caramelize one side then the other, watching closely. One coat is enough; put extra sauce on the table.

**Serve**

- Slice between the bones. The pink layer under the surface is the smoke ring, not undercooking.

**Notes**

- Meathead skips the Texas Crutch (foil wrap) at home; if you try it, no more than an hour, with about 1 fl oz water.
- With a salted bottled rub, skip the salt step.
