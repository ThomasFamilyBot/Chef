**Caramel**

- Have the fish sauce measured and next to the stove: once the caramel turns mahogany it goes in right away to stop the cooking.
- In a 12-inch skillet over medium-high heat (pan surface 375-425°F), combine 1 TBLS coconut water and the sugar. Bring to a boil and cook, stirring occasionally, until golden at the edges, about 3 minutes.
- Reduce to medium (300-350°F) and keep cooking, swirling the pan but not stirring, until the caramel is mahogany and gives off its first wisps, another 4-5 minutes.

**Braise**

- Off the heat, add the fish sauce and stir; it will steam and bubble hard.
- Set the pan over medium, pour in the remaining 1/2 cup coconut water and stir until fully incorporated. Add the shallots and ginger and bring to a simmer, then cover, reduce to low and cook 5 minutes.

**Chicken**

- Stir in the chicken. Cover and cook over medium, stirring once or twice, until a skewer meets no resistance, 15-20 minutes, adjusting the heat to keep a steady simmer (185-205°F).
- Uncover, raise to medium-high and simmer vigorously (big bubbles), stirring occasionally, until the chicken is glazed and the sauce is syrupy, about 8 minutes.

**Finish**

- Off the heat, stir in half the cilantro, the jalapeño, lime zest and lime juice. Top with the rest of the cilantro and serve with steamed rice and lime wedges.

**Notes**

- Nuoc mau, the dark, smoky caramel, flavors many Vietnamese dishes; this braising liquid is also great with fish.
