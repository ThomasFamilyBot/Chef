**Get the cookies ready**

- Cut the 12 cookies from `1 -> Mix-ins` into about 6 chunks each with a knife. Cut, don't crush: crumbs turn the dough gray.
- Lift the chunks into a bowl with your fingers and leave the dust on the cutting board. Add the chocolate chips to the same bowl.
- Break the 4 cookies from `2 -> Tops` into 6 pieces each and put them in a second bowl.
- Freeze both bowls while you make the dough, at least 15 minutes. Cold chunks hold their shape and the creme doesn't smear.

**Mix the dry ingredients**

- Whisk everything from `3 -> Dry` in a medium bowl.

**Beat the butter and sugar**

- Put everything from `4 -> Butter and sugar` in the stand mixer with the paddle. Beat on medium until pale and fluffy, 2 to 3 minutes. Scrape down the bowl.
- The butter is ready when a finger leaves a dent but doesn't sink in, about 65°F. Melty butter makes flat cookies.

**Add the egg and vanilla**

- Add everything from `5 -> Egg and vanilla`. Beat on medium until smooth, about 1 minute. Scrape down the bowl.

**Make the dough**

- Add the dry mix. Mix on low just until a few streaks of flour are left. Stop the mixer.
- Add the frozen chunks and chips from `1 -> Mix-ins`. Fold them in by hand with a spatula, only until the flour streaks are gone. The mixer would smash the cookies.

**Scoop and chill**

- Line two half sheet pans with parchment paper.
- Scoop the dough into 24 balls, about 2 heaping TBLS (45 g) each. Leave them tall; don't flatten them.
- Press 1 frozen piece from `2 -> Tops` onto the top of each ball.
- Chill the dough balls in the refrigerator for 30 minutes. Turn the oven on to 350°F now, with a rack in the middle, so it is fully hot when the dough is cold.

**Bake**

- Put 12 dough balls on each pan, about 3 inches apart.
- Bake one pan at a time on the middle rack for 10 to 12 minutes (est.). Keep the other pan in the refrigerator until its turn.
- They are done when the edges are set and barely golden and the middles are puffy and still look a little underdone. Take them out pale. They finish cooking on the hot pan.
- Bake 3 cookies first as a test. If the middles are doughy after they cool, add a minute. If they come out crunchy, take a minute off.

**Cool**

- Leave the cookies on the pan for 5 minutes, then move them to a rack.
- For round cookies, set a big glass or round cutter over each hot cookie and swirl it in little circles right when they come out.

**Notes**

- Annika's three choices: white vanilla dough, soft and chewy, and chocolate chips with the Double Stuf Oreo chunks.
- This is a new recipe and has not been test-baked yet. The bake time and the number of cookies are estimates; tell ChefBot how the first batch went and the recipe gets updated.
- Why they stay soft: butter, a little brown sugar, an extra egg yolk and a short bake keep cookies soft and chewy ([King Arthur Baking, Cookie Chemistry](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2016/03/14/cookie-chemistry-2)). The dough is mostly white sugar so it stays pale; all brown sugar would be even chewier but tan.
- Keep them in a container with a lid for 3 to 4 days. They are softest on the first day.
- Dough balls can be frozen in a zip bag. Bake them from frozen and add 1 to 2 minutes.
- Good crumbled over a scoop of Annika Cookies n Crème ice cream.
