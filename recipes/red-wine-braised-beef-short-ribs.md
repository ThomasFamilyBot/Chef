**Sear the ribs**

- Heat the oven to 300°F. Season the short ribs all over with salt and pepper.
- Heat the oil in a large Dutch oven over medium-high heat (pan surface 375-425°F) until shimmering.
- Working in batches, sear the ribs on all sides, about 4 minutes per side. Move them to a platter.

**Cook the aromatics**

- Pour off all but 2 TBLS of fat. Add the celery, carrots, onion and garlic from `2 -> Aromatics` and cook, stirring, until well colored, about 6 minutes.
- Stir in the tomato paste and cook 1 minute more. Turn the heat down any time the pot threatens to burn.

**Braise**

- Add the red wine and scrape up everything stuck to the pot. Bring to a simmer (185-205°F).
- If using store-bought stock, pour it into a bowl and sprinkle the gelatin over the top until it blooms. Add the stock to the pot.
- Return the ribs and their juices, nestling them into the liquid. Add the thyme and bay leaves.
- Cover partially and braise in the oven until fork-tender, 2 to 3 hours.

**Make the port syrup**

- While the ribs braise, bring the port to a very gentle simmer in a large saucepan. Reduce, uncovered, until syrupy, about 1 hour. You want about 1/2 cup.

**Reduce the sauce**

- Lift the ribs carefully onto a clean platter and tent with foil. They are very tender, so hold the bones in place as you move them.
- Skim the fat from the braising liquid. Strain the liquid through a fine-mesh strainer, pressing on the vegetables, and discard the solids.
- Rinse the Dutch oven and return the strained liquid to it. Simmer gently until reduced to 2 cups, about 1 hour, skimming any foam.
- Stir in the port syrup.

**Finish**

- The sauce should coat the back of a spoon and hold a trail when you drag a finger through it. If it is still thin, whisk in the cornstarch slurry from `5 -> Finish` and simmer until slightly thickened.
- Season with salt and pepper. Return the ribs to the pot and spoon the sauce over and around them to glaze and rewarm. Serve.

**Notes**

- Make ahead: the ribs keep in their finished sauce, in an airtight container in the fridge, for up to 5 days. Reheat gently.
- Flanken-cut ribs also work if each slab is about 2 inches thick; cut between the bones into portions.
- Serve with mashed potatoes, polenta or crusty bread. Off the bone and shredded into the sauce, it also dresses fresh tagliatelle or pappardelle.
- Readers on the recipe page suggest making the port syrup a day ahead and adding it to taste, since the full amount can be sweet.
