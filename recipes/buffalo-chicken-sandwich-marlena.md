**Up to a week ahead: fermented cabbage**

- Salt the julienned cabbage with enough salt to cover and let sit 6 hours at room temperature. Rinse in cold water and squeeze dry.
- In a sanitized glass or plastic container, combine the cabbage with fresh salt (5% of the cabbage's weight) and water to cover (6% of its weight). Lay plastic wrap on the surface so it stays submerged and cover loosely so gas can escape. Ferment at room temperature up to a week, to taste. (Coleslaw, or shredded cabbage tossed in the aioli, is the shortcut.)

**The day before: chicken brine**

- Bring 2 quarts water, 3 TBLS salt and the palm sugar to a boil. Off the heat, add the smashed garlic and rosemary and cool completely, to 40°F or below.
- Pour the brine over the thighs in a container that holds them. After brining, set the thighs on a rack in the fridge to air-dry at least 24 hours for crisp skin.

**Pickles**

- Boil the vinegar, 3/4 cup water, sugar and 1 1/2 TBLS salt. Pour over the cucumber rounds in a heatproof jar. Sit at least 4 hours.

**Buffalo sauce**

- In a saucepan over medium-high, whisk the hot sauce and cold butter until fully emulsified; don't overheat or it breaks. Hold warm. Keeps a week refrigerated.

**Aioli**

- In a food processor (or bowl and whisk), blend the yolks, minced garlic, half the lemon juice and a good pinch of salt for 30 seconds. Slowly drizzle in the grapeseed oil.
- When it thickens about halfway through, add the rest of the lemon juice to loosen it, then finish the oil. Add the Espelette and adjust the salt.

**Dredge and fry**

- Heat the frying oil to 350°F in a heavy pot or fryer. Whisk half the cornstarch with 1 cup water into a smooth slurry.
- Dredge the chicken in the dry cornstarch, dip in the slurry, and fry at 350°F until 170°F internal.
- Rest 3 minutes on paper towels.

**Assemble**

- Toast the buns. Spread about 3 TBLS aioli on each.
- Toss the chicken in a liberal amount of buffalo sauce, covering the whole thigh. Set on the bun with about 3 TBLS cabbage and 7 pickle slices. Serve hot.

**Notes**

- The sauce, aioli, pickles and cabbage all make more than six sandwiches need; nutrition counts the portions used (see the line notes).
- The source lists 6 egg yolks but the method says "the egg yolk"; six is right for this much oil.
