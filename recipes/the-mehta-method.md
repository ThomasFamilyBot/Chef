**The day before**

- Put the ice cream maker's freeze bowl in the freezer. It needs 24 hours.

**Toast the coconut**

- Heat the oven to 325°F. Spread the coconut from `1 -> Toasted coconut` on a baking sheet.
- Bake 6 to 8 minutes, stirring halfway, until the edges are golden. Watch it: it turns fast.
- Cool completely, then freeze in a small container.

**Make the vanilla coconut custard**

- In a saucepan, combine the coconut milk, sugar and salt from `2 -> Vanilla coconut custard`. Warm over medium heat (pan surface 300-350°F), stirring, until the sugar dissolves and it steams. Do not let it bubble.
- Whisk the egg yolks in a separate bowl. Drizzle in 1/2 cup of the warm coconut milk, whisking, to temper them.
- Pour the yolk mixture back into the pan. Cook over medium-low, stirring constantly, until it reaches 170-175°F and lightly coats a spatula. Don't go past 180°F or the eggs scramble.
- Pour the cold cream into a large bowl and set a fine mesh strainer over it. Strain the custard into the cream. Stir in the vanilla.
- Chill until completely cold: 4 hours minimum, overnight preferred.
- Put the tub the ice cream will live in into the freezer.

**Melt the chocolate** (just before churning)

- Melt the chocolate and coconut oil from `3 -> Chocolate shards` together, in the microwave in 20-second bursts or over a pan of hot water. Stir until smooth.
- Let it cool until barely warm but still pourable.

**Churn**

- Start the ice cream maker first, then pour in the cold custard. Churn until thick, like soft serve, 20 to 30 minutes in the KitchenAid bowl.
- With the machine still turning, drizzle in the chocolate in a thin, slow stream. It freezes on contact and breaks into shards.
- Add the frozen toasted coconut and let it turn a few more times, just until mixed in.

**The Mehta Method: layer in the caramel**

- If the dulce de leche is too stiff to drizzle, warm it for 10 seconds. It should be loose, not hot.
- Spread one third of the ice cream in the cold tub. Drizzle on one third of the caramel from `4 -> Caramel ribbon` and add a tiny pinch of flaky salt.
- Do that two more times, ending with caramel on top.
- Drag a butter knife through once to pull the caramel into ribbons. Don't stir.

**Freeze**

- Cover and freeze until firm, at least 6 hours.

**Notes**

- Where each flavor lives: vanilla in the custard, coconut in the custard and the toasted flakes, chocolate in the shards, caramel in the ribbons.
- This is a new recipe and has not been made yet. The custard is the library's Vanilla Ice Cream with coconut milk in place of the whole milk and a little less sugar, since the coconut and caramel are sweet. Treat the first batch as the test and tell ChefBot what to change.
- The coconut oil is what makes the chocolate set thin and snap. Without it the chocolate freezes into hard, waxy bits.
- Sweetened shredded coconut stays pleasantly chewy when frozen. Unsweetened flakes are crunchier and can be hard.
- One freeze bowl makes one batch. Give it 24 hours back in the freezer before churning another ice cream.
