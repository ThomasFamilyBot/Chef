**Sear the ribs**

- Heat the oven to 300°F. Cut the boneless ribs into chunks about the size of the bone-in pieces. Season all the short ribs with salt and pepper.
- Heat the oil in a large Dutch oven over medium-high heat (pan surface 375-425°F) until shimmering.
- Working in batches, sear the ribs on all sides, about 4 minutes per side. Move them to a platter.

**Cook the aromatics**

- Pour off all but 2 TBLS of fat. Add the celery, carrots, onion and garlic from `3 -> Aromatics` and cook, stirring, until well colored, about 6 minutes.
- Stir in the tomato paste from `4 -> Tomato paste` and cook 1 minute more. Turn the heat down any time the pot threatens to burn.

**Braise**

- Add the red wine from `5 -> Deglaze` on its own and scrape up everything stuck to the pot. Bring to a simmer (185-205°F) before anything else goes in.
- If using store-bought stock, pour it into a bowl and sprinkle the gelatin over the top until it blooms (`6 -> Stock`). Add the stock to the pot.
- Return the ribs and their juices, nestling them into the liquid. Add the thyme and bay leaves from `7 -> Herbs`.
- Cover partially and braise in the oven until fork-tender, 2 to 3 hours. Start checking at 2 hours: the boneless chunks may be ready before the bone-in pieces. Lift pieces onto a platter as they turn fork-tender and let the rest finish.

**Make the port syrup**

- While the ribs braise, bring the port to a very gentle simmer in a large saucepan: 180-190°F, with a small bubble breaking the surface every second or two and steady steam. Reduce, uncovered, until syrupy, about 1 hour. You want about 1/2 cup.
- Don't let it boil hard; that dulls the port and makes it easy to scorch. If it is barely moving after 20 minutes, nudge it up to 195-200°F.
- Turn the burner down as it thickens. The last 10 to 15 minutes are when it can burn. It thickens more as it cools.

**Reduce the sauce**

- Lift the ribs carefully onto a clean platter and tent with foil. They are very tender, so hold the bones in place as you move them.
- Skim the fat from the braising liquid. Strain the liquid through a fine-mesh strainer, pressing on the vegetables, and discard the solids.
- Rinse the Dutch oven and return the strained liquid to it. Simmer gently until reduced to 2 cups, about 1 hour, skimming any foam.
- Stir in the port syrup.

**Finish**

- The sauce should coat the back of a spoon and hold a trail when you drag a finger through it. If it is still thin, whisk in the cornstarch slurry from `9 -> Slurry` and simmer until slightly thickened.
- Season with salt and pepper. Return the ribs to the pot and spoon the sauce over and around them to glaze and rewarm. Serve.

**Notes**

- Thanksgiving 26 version. What changed from the original: 2 lb bone-in English-cut ribs plus 2 lb boneless chuck short ribs stand in for 5 lb of bone-in ribs. That is about the same amount of meat, so every liquid is as written.
- Both cuts came from the freezer, so they are left off the shopping list. Trim any dry, pale freezer-burned spots before searing.
- Make it Monday to Wednesday. The ribs keep in their finished sauce, in an airtight container in the fridge, for up to 5 days. Rewarm gently on the stove on Thursday; the oven stays free.
- Readers on the recipe page suggest making the port syrup a day ahead and adding it to taste, since the full amount can be sweet. It keeps in a jar in the fridge, and the reduction can be stopped and restarted without harm.
- The 180-190°F target for the port is ours; the source says only "a very gentle simmer".
