**Check the lamb** (when it has thawed)

- Open the pack and smell it. It should smell clean. If the fat smells stale or waxy, trim the fat off and smell again; if it still does, use a fresh loin.

**Salt and tie** (Wednesday night)

- Salt the loin all over with the salt from `1 -> Salt and tie`.
- If the loin has a tenderloin or flap attached, roll it over them. With butcher's twine, tie at 1-inch intervals so it holds an even, round shape.
- Refrigerate overnight. Take it out about 45 minutes before cooking.
- This is the same prep for the fire and for the skillet, so the choice can wait until Thursday.

**Board sauce** (before the cook)

- Chop the sage, thyme and jalapeño from `2 -> Board sauce` into a coffee cup. Add the garlic and 1/4 tsp pepper.
- Drizzle in 6 TBLS of the olive oil. Let it sit while the lamb cooks.

**Smoke low**

- Set up the fire for two zones and bring the grill to 225°F. Use only a little wood: the cook is short and the loin is mild.
- Brush the lamb and the grate with the remaining 1 TBLS oil. Season the lamb with pepper.
- Put the lamb on the indirect side and cook until it reads 110°F internal. For a loin this size expect about 30 to 45 minutes.

**Finish over the flame**

- Move the lamb over direct heat (grill grate 500°F+). Sear all sides, 2 to 3 minutes per side, without moving it in between.
- Take it off at 125-130°F internal. It climbs to 130-135°F, medium-rare, on the board.

**Board and carve**

- Pour the board sauce onto the cutting board. Set the lamb on the oiled herbs and coat all sides.
- Cut off the strings and slice right away, across the grain, about 1/2 inch thick. Roll the slices in the juices before serving.

**If it storms: skillet**

- Mix 1 TBLS of the extra-virgin olive oil with the garlic, shallot, rosemary and marjoram from `4 -> Skillet fallback`, plus a pinch each of the chopped sage and thyme. Rub it all over the tied loin.
- Heat the oven to 350°F. In a 10-inch skillet (cast iron if you have it), heat the other 1 TBLS oil until shimmering.
- Cook the loin over moderate heat (pan surface 300-350°F), turning, until colored all over, about 12 to 15 minutes.
- Move the skillet to the oven until the lamb reads 125-130°F. Start checking after 5 minutes. Rest 10 minutes, then carve.
- The board sauce still works indoors: carve the lamb on it the same way.

**Notes**

- Thanksgiving 26 version. What changed from the original: one 1 1/2 lb boneless loin in place of two (3 lb), cooked over the fire by default with the board sauce from the library's Grilled Leg of Lamb. The original's skillet method, with its herb paste halved, is the fallback for bad weather.
- The fire times are estimates for one small loin; the leg recipe they come from takes about 2 1/2 hours. Go by the thermometer.
- The board sauce is a third of the leg recipe's.
- The loin came from the freezer, so it is left off the shopping list. The fallback ingredients are on the list so they are in the house either way.
- Chile for the board sauce: a green jalapeño or a Fresno chile works in place of red, same amount. A serrano is two to three times hotter, so use a quarter of one. Not habanero: far hotter, and its fruity flavor takes over the herbs.
- No fresh thyme: make the board sauce with 2 TBLS chopped flat-leaf parsley plus 1 tsp dried thyme, and let it sit in the oil for the whole cook. Dried thyme alone tastes dusty in a raw sauce.
- A "poultry blend" herb pack is usually thyme, sage and rosemary, which covers this recipe.
- Indoors, the 350°F oven is the same temperature the stuffing and the mac and cheese bake at.
