**Check the lamb**

- After it thaws, open the pack and smell it. It should smell clean. If the fat smells stale or waxy, trim the fat off and smell again; if it still does, use a fresh loin.

**Herb paste**

- In a small bowl, combine 1 TBLS of the oil with the garlic, shallot, rosemary, sage, marjoram and thyme.

**Roll and tie**

- Lay the loin on a work surface, fat side down, and season with salt and pepper. Spread the herb paste all over the lamb.
- If the loin has a tenderloin or flap attached, roll it over them into a neat roulade. If it is a single trimmed piece, leave it as it is.
- With butcher's twine, tie at 1-inch intervals so it holds a round shape. Season the outside with salt and pepper.
- This can be done on Wednesday. Keep it wrapped in the fridge and take it out about 45 minutes before cooking.

**Brown and roast**

- Heat the oven to 350°F. In a 10-inch skillet (cast iron if you have it), heat the remaining 1 TBLS oil until shimmering.
- Add the loin and cook over moderate heat (pan surface 300-350°F), turning, until browned all over. One loin takes less time than the original's two: expect about 12 to 15 minutes.
- Move the skillet to the oven and roast until an instant-read thermometer in the thickest part reads 125-130°F for medium-rare. Start checking after 5 minutes. It climbs about 5°F as it rests.
- Move the loin to a carving board and rest 10 minutes.

**Serve**

- Cut off the strings and carve into slices about 1/2 inch thick, so there are two slices for everyone.

**Notes**

- Thanksgiving 26 version. What changed from the original: one 1 1/2 lb boneless loin in place of two loins (3 lb), with the herb paste halved. The skillet oil is not halved.
- The browning and roasting times here are estimates for one smaller roast; the original gives 20 minutes of browning and about 10 minutes in the oven for two loins. Go by the thermometer.
- The loin came from the freezer, so it is left off the shopping list.
- The 350°F oven is the same temperature the stuffing and the mac and cheese bake at.
