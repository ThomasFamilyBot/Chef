**Toast the milk powder**

- Tip the milk powder into a cold skillet over medium-low heat (pan surface 250-300°F). Cook, stirring constantly to break up clumps, until it's the golden brown of a graham cracker, about 5 minutes.
- Tip it onto a plate right away to stop the cooking.

**Toast the vanilla bean**

- Hold the bean with tongs over a medium gas flame (or use a kitchen torch) until fragrant and just starting to char; it puffs and turns shiny. A couple of minutes. No gas? Press it against a ripping-hot dry cast-iron pan.
- Cool, split lengthwise, and scrape out the seeds. Keep both seeds and pod.

**Yolk blend**

- In a blender, combine 40 g of the sugar, the milk, dextrose, yolks, toasted milk powder, vanilla seeds, salt and locust bean gum (if using). Pulse until smooth and the yolks are fully dispersed.
- (With an immersion blender: skip this and blend everything directly in the pan, heat off, after the caramel and cream are combined.)

**Caramel and custard**

- In a saucepan of at least 3 quarts over medium heat (pan surface 300-350°F), spread the remaining 50 g sugar in an even layer. Don't stir; once it starts melting at the edges, gently push the liquid sugar toward the center until it's all melted and golden brown, graham-cracker color (about 340°F).
- Slowly drizzle in the cream to stop the caramel; it sputters and steams. Bring to a simmer and stir to dissolve; any shards melt as it cooks.
- Add the yolk blend and the vanilla pod. Cook over medium heat, stirring often, until the custard reaches 180°F or coats the back of a spoon.
- Off the heat, cover and steep 20 minutes.
- Cool the base in an ice bath, then cover and refrigerate overnight, at least 8 hours.

**Churn**

- Fish out the vanilla pod. Churn per your machine's directions to a soft-serve consistency.
- Pack into an airtight container and freeze at least 4 hours. Let soften a few minutes before scooping.

**Notes**

- The milk powder isn't optional; it's one of the three toasts.
- Source gives no yield; the base is about 4 cups, so figure a quart.
