**Day 1**

**Caramelized cornflakes** (makes about 18 oz)

- Heat the oven to 275°F.
- Put the cornflakes in a large bowl and crush them a few times with your hands. In another bowl, stir together the milk powder, sugar and salt.
- Add the melted butter to the cornflakes, then the sugar mixture, and toss to coat.
- Spread on two parchment-lined baking sheets and bake until caramelized and deep golden, about 35 minutes. Cool to room temperature.

**Cereal milk** (makes about 1 cup)

- Combine 14 oz of the caramelized cornflakes with the milk in a large bowl and steep 1 hour. Save the remaining 4 oz for serving.
- Strain through a fine-mesh sieve, pressing on the flakes with a spatula to get out as much liquid as you can. Strain again through a finer sieve or cheesecloth. Don't taste it yet; it's meant to be intense.

**Custard**

- Pour the heavy cream into a medium bowl and refrigerate.
- Combine the cereal milk, sugar, salt and vanilla in a small pot and bring to a boil over medium heat.
- In a medium pot, whisk the yolks. Slowly pour in the warm milk mixture, whisking constantly.
- Set over medium heat and stir until the custard coats the back of a wooden spoon (170-180°F).
- Pour through a strainer into the chilled cream and stir to combine. Chill overnight.

**Day 2**

**Churn**

- Freeze in the ice cream machine per the manufacturer's instructions.
- Serve with the reserved caramelized cornflakes.

**Notes**

- The milk powder in the cornflake coating is what makes the flavor read as cereal, not just toast; keep it in.
- Nutrition counts the dairy and custard in full, but only about 30% of the cornflake coating and 25% of the milk, since the steeped flakes and the milk they absorb are discarded.
