**Caramel**

- Set the measured cream by the stove. Measure out 1/2 cup of the sugar for the caramel; the rest goes with the yolks.
- Put 2 TBLS of the caramel sugar in a heavy stainless pan over medium-high heat (pan surface 375-425°F). When it melts at the edges and turns amber in places, about 2 minutes, stir gently and add another 2 TBLS.
- Keep adding the rest of the 1/2 cup, 2 TBLS at a time, letting most of it melt before the next addition, stirring gently so it melts evenly.
- When the caramel is dark mahogany (about 350-360°F), take the pan off the heat and immediately pour in the cream slowly. It steams and bubbles up; wear oven mitts. When it subsides, stir until smooth. Hardened lumps melt out over low heat.

**Base**

- Once the caramel is smooth, stir in the milk and salt and set over medium-high heat. As it approaches a bare simmer (180-190°F), reduce to medium.

**Yolks and custard**

- In a medium heatproof bowl, whisk the yolks just to break them up, then whisk in the remaining 1/4 cup sugar.
- Whisking constantly, add about 1/2 cup of the hot cream to the yolks; repeat with another 1/2 cup. Stirring the pan with a heatproof spatula, slowly pour the yolk mixture back in.
- Cook over medium heat, stirring constantly, until thickened enough to coat the spatula and hold a clear line when you run a finger through it, 1-2 minutes more (170-180°F).
- Strain into a clean container and cool over an ice bath, stirring now and then. Cover and refrigerate at least 2 hours or overnight.

**Freeze**

- Churn the cold base per your machine's instructions. Chill the storage container in the freezer meanwhile.
- Eat right away, or freeze at least 4 hours for a firmer scoop.

**Notes**

- Bitter, complex caramel against a rich custard; take the caramel darker than feels safe.
