**Day 1**

**Toast the milk powder**

- Spread the milk powder in a single layer on a sheet pan and bake at 325°F, stirring often, until deeply golden, the color of graham crackers. It burns easily, so keep an eye on it. (A dry skillet over medium-low heat, stirring constantly, works too.)

**Infuse the dairy**

- In a medium saucepan, whisk the milk, cream and toasted milk powder. Cook over medium heat (pan surface 300-350°F), whisking often so it doesn't scorch, until steaming.
- Off the heat, cover and cool to room temperature. Refrigerate and infuse at least 12 hours, preferably 24.

**Day 2**

**Strain and weigh the dairy**

- Weigh an empty container. Set a fine-mesh strainer lined with a double layer of cheesecloth over it and strain the dairy, then gather the cheesecloth and gently squeeze the solids to get out as much liquid as you can without letting solids through. Discard the solids.
- Weigh the container again. You need 700 g of infused dairy; add cream to make up the difference.

**Custard base**

- In a medium saucepan, whisk the sugar, xanthan gum and salt. Add the corn syrup and yolks and whisk until combined. Stream in the infused dairy and whisk until smooth.
- Cook over medium-low heat, stirring and scraping constantly with a flexible spatula, until the custard is hot to the touch and coats the back of a spoon, 170-180°F.
- Strain into a heat-safe container and stir in the vanilla. Cover and chill at least 4 hours (or over an ice bath).

**Churn and freeze**

- Put a freezer-safe container (a loaf pan works) in the freezer.
- Churn the chilled base per your machine's instructions until it looks like soft serve, 20-25 minutes.
- Pack into the chilled container, press parchment onto the surface, lid it, and freeze until firm. Keeps up to 3 months.

**Notes**

- No xanthan gum? Whisk 5 g tapioca starch with 20 g cold milk and stir it in after the custard reaches temperature, before straining.
- No corn syrup? Use the same weight of sugar; the result is sweeter and a bit icier.
- Use nonfat milk powder: the point is milk solids, not extra fat.
