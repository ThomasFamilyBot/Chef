**Prep**

- Peel and set aside 1 garlic clove for the mayo; peel and smash the other 6.
- Pick the leaves from the celery heart; slice the heart crosswise into half-moons until you have about 1 1/4 cups. Set aside 1/4 cup plus the leaves for serving.
- Peel the ginger. Set aside a 1/2-inch nub; slice the rest thinly.
- Rinse the mussels in cold water and pull off the beards. Discard any open ones that don't close when tapped. Drain into a large bowl.

**Broth**

- Melt the butter in a large pot or Dutch oven over medium heat (pan surface 300-350°F). Add the smashed garlic, sliced ginger and 1 cup celery and cook, stirring often, until the garlic is golden, 5-8 minutes.
- Add the mirin, sambal and 1/4 cup soy sauce and bring to a simmer (185-205°F). Cook, stirring once or twice, until reduced by about half, about 5 minutes.

**Tomatoes**

- Add the tomatoes and their juice, breaking them up with your hands as they go in. Bring to a simmer, season lightly with salt, and simmer uncovered about 5 minutes.

**Mussels**

- Stir the mussels into the tomato mixture. Cover and cook, stirring once halfway, until opened and cooked through, 6-8 minutes. Discard any that stay shut.

**Garlic-ginger mayo**

- Meanwhile, grate the reserved garlic clove and ginger nub into a small bowl. Stir in the mayonnaise and the remaining 1 TBLS soy sauce.

**Serve**

- Heat the broiler with a rack at the top. Toast the baguette halves cut side up directly on the rack until golden, about 1 minute. Cool.
- Ladle the mussels and broth into shallow bowls. Top with the reserved chopped celery and leaves.
- Tear the bread into pieces, smear the toasted sides with the mayo, and serve for soaking up the broth.

**Notes**

- Mussels are counted at the whole-shell weight with the table's edible yield; see the nutrition lines.
