**Make the Rice Krispie treats** (up to 2 days ahead)

- Melt the butter in a large stockpot over medium-low heat (pan surface 250-300°F).
- Add the marshmallows and stir until melted. Off the heat, stir in the Rice Krispies 2 cups at a time.
- Press into a 9x13-inch pan and cool to room temperature.
- Pull half the pan apart into bite-size pieces for the ice cream. The other half is yours.

**Make the ice cream base**

- Separate the eggs; reserve 3 of the whites for the fluff.
- In a medium heatproof bowl, whisk the 4 yolks with 1/4 cup of the sugar about 1 minute, until slightly thickened. Set aside.
- Melt the butter in a large heavy saucepan over medium heat (pan surface 300-350°F) until the foam subsides and the solids start to separate from the golden liquid.
- Whisk in the cream and cook 2-3 minutes to warm it. Whisk in the milk, remaining 1/4 cup sugar and the salt.
- Stir often until the liquid steams and bubbles at the edges, then reduce the heat to low.
- Drizzle 1/2 cup of the hot base into the yolks, whisking, then slowly whisk the yolks into the pot.
- Stir constantly with a silicone spatula 3-5 minutes more, until the custard coats the spatula in sheets rather than ribbons (170-180°F).
- Strain into a clean bowl, stir in the vanilla, cover, and chill at least 4 hours or overnight.
- Churn in an ice cream maker per the manufacturer's instructions.

**While it churns, make the marshmallow fluff**

- Whisk the 3 egg whites and the sugar 1-2 minutes in a double boiler (or a bowl over a pan of simmering water) until the sugar dissolves and the mixture is warm.
- Move to a stand mixer and whisk on medium-high about 5 minutes to a thick, stiff fluff, adding the cream of tartar after about 2 minutes, as the whites turn from translucent to opaque.

**Assemble**

- Working quickly, scoop the churned ice cream into a very large bowl.
- Stir in the fluff and the Rice Krispie treat pieces; the ice cream softens as you go, so move fast.
- Pack into freezer containers and freeze at least 4 hours before serving.

- Nutrition counts half the treats (the half that goes in).
